Bahira Foundation is a small welfare organisation in Bahawalpur, in South Punjab. We help people in need, and we speak up for those who are left out.

## A note from our founder {#founder}

I started Bahira Foundation on {founded}, Human Rights Day, to brighten lives and restore hope: in small, practical ways, starting with the people around me in Bahawalpur.

So far we have done three things: a day with the children at Daykh Bhaal Shelter Home, an iftar at Bahawalpur Railway Station, and a Sabeel in Mohalla Nawaban. I paid for each of them myself, and friends, family and people who saw our posts came to help.

Bahira is small, but it is meant to grow. Once donations come in, I want to build a proper team of volunteers and hold an event at least once a month. And I want us to grow into the rest of our work: speaking up for women and intersex people, caring for stray animals, planting trees, and teaching young people modern skills.

This journey begins in Bahawalpur, but our vision knows no bounds. Join us as we spread light, love and opportunity.

## Our name and our sign {#name}

Bahira comes from the Arabic word {arabic}, which means luminous: bright, giving light. That is what we hope to be for the people around us.

Our sign is one shape with four ideas in it.

### The B

Bahira’s first letter is the outline of the whole sign.

### The flame on top

Light, which is what the name means.

### The lamp

At the base is a chiragh, the everyday oil lamp that keeps a home lit.

### The flame in the lamp

A second, smaller light, carried inside the B.

## Why we began on Human Rights Day {#human-rights-day}

The world marks Human Rights Day on 10 December, and that is the day we chose to begin. Almost everything we do is for people: food for those who are hungry, safety and fair treatment for women, dignity for intersex people, trees for a better future, and skills for young people.

Caring for stray animals is the one part of our work that is not about people.

## What we want {#mission}

### Our mission

To help people in need, and to speak up for those who are left out: women, intersex people and the animals on our streets. To plant trees, and to give young people modern skills. All of it starting in Bahawalpur.

### Our hope

A South Punjab where help reaches the people who need it, where women and intersex people live with dignity, where animals are treated kindly, and where young people can put their talents to use. We want to spread light, love and opportunity as far as we can reach.

## What we hold to {#values}

### Dignity first

We help people without making them feel small. We never use anyone’s hardship to get attention, and we never share photos that show people as helpless.

### Honest about what we do

We only share what really happened, with the date, the place and real photos. We say plainly what we have not done yet.

### Small, but done properly

A meal, a cold drink, a day with children. We do what we can afford, and we do it well.

### Open to everyone

We help people whoever they are. Anyone who shares these values is welcome to join us.

## Where we work {#where}

We are based in Bahawalpur, in South Punjab, and so far all of our work has been here: at a shelter home, at the railway station and in Mohalla Nawaban. As Bahira grows, we want to reach further across South Punjab.

## Who runs Bahira {#who}

Bahira is run by its founder, {founder}. There is no permanent team yet: volunteers join us for each event, through social media, family and friends.

Bahira is not registered yet. We plan to register with the Punjab Charity Commission. To see how Bahira is run and paid for, read our [accountability page](/accountability/).
